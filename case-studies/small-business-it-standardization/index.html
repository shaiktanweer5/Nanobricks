Nanobricks Case Study

Standardizing IT for a Growing Small Business

How Nanobricks helped bring structure, consistency and security to a small office IT environment by standardizing endpoints, access controls, business applications and shared storage.

Request IT Assessment Explore Managed IT Services

Project Overview

A practical IT standardization engagement for a growing small-business office environment in Hyderabad.

Environment

Small-business office environment.

Endpoint Scope

Approximately 10 business computers.

Focus

Standardization, access control and endpoint security.

Location

Hyderabad, Telangana.

The Challenge

The business relied on multiple office computers for routine work such as documents, spreadsheets, PDF files, email, printing and shared business data.

Over time, the environment had developed inconsistencies across devices, software, user access and system usage.

Key areas requiring improvement

  • Inconsistent workstation configurations.
  • Different software and operating-system setups across devices.
  • Limited standardization of user accounts and device naming.
  • Shared business information requiring controlled access.
  • Need for better endpoint-security practices.
  • Requirement to identify older systems that should be upgraded or replaced.
  • Need for a repeatable IT baseline that could be supported more easily.

What Nanobricks Assessed

Before making changes, the environment was reviewed to understand how devices, users and business information were being used.

Endpoint Health

Reviewed desktop and laptop condition, hardware capability and suitability for continued business use.

Windows Baseline

Reviewed operating-system readiness and created a consistent Windows configuration approach.

Business Applications

Identified requirements for Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF applications and browsers.

User Access

Reviewed how users accessed systems and business information, including shared and restricted data.

Peripheral Controls

Reviewed USB and removable-media requirements to determine where restrictions were appropriate.

Shared Data

Reviewed how shared business files were stored and how different users should access them.

What We Implemented

The environment was standardized around a practical baseline designed for day-to-day business use and easier ongoing support.

Standardized Device Naming

Business computers were organized using a consistent naming convention to make identification, support and administration easier.

Windows 11 Pro Baseline

A consistent Windows 11 Pro baseline was defined for supported business systems, including common configuration and security settings.

Microsoft Defender

Microsoft Defender and standard Windows security protections were included as part of the endpoint baseline.

Update Standardization

Windows and common business applications were aligned with a consistent update and maintenance approach.

Separate User Access

Separate system accounts and password controls were introduced to reduce unnecessary shared access.

USB Restrictions

Removable-media access could be restricted where business requirements required tighter control over data movement.

Shared Business Storage

Shared business storage was structured to support common working files while separating more sensitive information.

Permission-Based Access

Access requirements were defined so selected users could receive appropriate read or edit permissions for business data.

Application Standardization

Common business tools such as office applications, browsers, PDF software and printer support were standardized across systems.

Hardware Replacement Planning

Older or unsuitable systems were identified so the business could prioritize replacements instead of upgrading every device blindly.

Improving Security Without Overcomplicating IT

Small businesses often do not need a complex enterprise-security architecture to make meaningful improvements.

In this engagement, the focus was on practical controls that could be implemented and maintained consistently.

  • Standard operating-system baseline.
  • Endpoint protection using Microsoft Defender.
  • Controlled user access.
  • Reduced unnecessary use of shared credentials.
  • USB restrictions where appropriate.
  • Clearer permissions for business information.
  • Consistent patching and application maintenance.

Business Outcome

The project created a more consistent and support-ready IT environment for the business.

  • More consistent workstation configurations.
  • Easier identification and administration of office computers.
  • Better-defined user and file access.
  • Improved endpoint-security baseline.
  • Clearer hardware replacement priorities.
  • A repeatable IT standard that can support future growth.

The Key Lesson

Small-business IT does not need to be complicated. A consistent baseline for devices, users, applications, security and shared data can make the environment easier to operate, secure and support.
